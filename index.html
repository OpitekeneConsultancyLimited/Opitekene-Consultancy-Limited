CROSS-BORDER TAX

International tax, from a Uganda perspective.

Explore the cross-border issues that can affect people and businesses operating in or with Uganda.

International tax depends on the countries involved, the tax year, the contract and the facts. This guide is not a worldwide rate database and does not determine residence, treaty entitlement, permanent establishment, transfer-pricing compliance or filing obligations. Confirm current rules in every relevant country before acting.
01

Residence and source

Identify where a person or company is tax-resident, where income arises, and which countries may tax it. Citizenship, incorporation and payment location are not always decisive on their own.

02

Tax treaties and double taxation

Check whether an effective agreement applies to both parties and the income type, then review residence, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse and documentation requirements before seeking relief.

03

Cross-border withholding

Review dividends, interest, royalties, rent, services, management fees, transport, insurance and other payments to non-residents. Determine the tax base, rate, timing, reporting and whether withholding is creditable or final.

04

Permanent establishment

Assess fixed places of business, projects, dependent agents, service activity and other local presence under domestic law and any applicable treaty. A PE may change profit attribution, registration and return obligations.

05

Transfer pricing and related parties

Identify associated-party transactions, test arm’s-length pricing, keep contemporaneous evidence, and assess local documentation, disclosure, financing and adjustment rules.

06

Foreign income and double-tax relief

Review foreign-source income, tax paid abroad, available credit or exemption mechanisms, limits, proof and timing. Relief is not automatic and may be restricted by income category or law.

07

Cross-border employees

Consider work location and days, employer and economic-employer facts, payroll withholding, benefits, social security, immigration status, remote work and any treaty employment conditions.

08

VAT and digital services

Check imported services, reverse-charge VAT, non-resident digital supplies, e-commerce platforms, place-of-supply rules, registration, invoicing and recoverability in each country.

09

Customs and regional trade

Confirm HS classification, customs value, origin, EAC or other preferential rules, proof of origin, import VAT, excise, export controls and the destination country’s import obligations.

10

Groups, anti-avoidance and BEPS

Review controlled-foreign-company rules, interest limitations, hybrid arrangements, treaty abuse, country-by-country reporting and global minimum-tax rules where the group and jurisdiction are in scope.

11

Foreign exchange and payments

Track currency conversion, timing, withholding certificates, tax gross-ups, foreign-exchange gains or losses, bank evidence and payment/reporting rules for cross-border transactions.

12

Disclosure, audits and disputes

Keep contracts, invoices, travel/workday records, residency certificates, proof of tax paid, customs records and related-party support. Check deadlines, exchange-of-information exposure, objection routes and treaty mutual-agreement procedures.

WHAT CHANGED

          
FY 2026/27 updates

          
TaxComp reflects selected changes for the 2026/27 year of income. Its advisory and chatbot now index guidance against the 2025–2026 domestic tax-law compilation, covering income tax, VAT, tax procedures, international income, excise, stamp duty and gaming. The compilation warns that it is not a substitute for gazetted Acts. The Income Tax (Amendment) Act, 2026 and Excise Duty (Amendment) Act, 2026 are reported as enacted; proposals and final provisions can differ. This is a summary, not a complete amendment register.

        

        

          
01
VAT registration thresholds increased
UGX 300 million in annual taxable supplies or more than UGX 75 million in a three-month period.

          
02
VAT withholding narrowed
VAT withholding applies to relevant taxable-supply payments where the supplier has not issued an EFRIS e-invoice or e-receipt. Input-credit recovery is also affected by e-invoice evidence and exempt supplies.

          
03
Withholding expands to specified payments
Includes 6% on gross payments to public entertainers, 15% on betting winnings (payout less stake), 10% on specified telecom/mobile-money commissions and 5% on qualifying offshore debenture interest. Scope, payer, exclusions and effective dates depend on the Act.

          
04
Individuals may elect monthly provisional rental returns
The enacted-law commentary describes monthly provisional returns as an option, not a general mandatory monthly filing requirement. TaxComp's rental calculator remains an annual estimate; confirm the applicable filing route with URA.

          
05
Related-party arm’s-length rule stated expressly
The 2026 Income Tax amendment adds an express arm’s-length obligation for controlled transactions. Review transaction substance, pricing support and current documentation requirements.

          
06
Excise rates changed for specified goods
The 2026 Excise Duty amendment changes some product-specific rates, including fuel, cement, sugar, cooking oil and plastics. TaxComp does not assume a rate: verify the exact goods, tariff and current statutory schedule.

          
Sources: URA (official tax information) · MRT Tax, August 2026 enacted-law commentary (secondary source). Confirm the enacted Acts and commencement provisions before applying a rule.
